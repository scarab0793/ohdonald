Taxes · Feb 25, 2016 · Misleading

Tax returns: from 'certainly' to audit excuses

WHEN & WHERE WAS IT SAID?

When: Feb 25, 2016

Where: Republican primary debate

Original context / reporting ↗
THE CLAIM (BRACE YOURSELF)
He couldn't release tax returns while being audited

WHAT HE SAID (WORD FOR WORD, SADLY)

At the debate he said, "I'm being audited now for two or three years, so I can't do it until the audit is finished, obviously."

WHEN AND WHERE (THE RECEIPTS HAVE A CALENDAR)

Republican debate, Feb. 25, 2016. A year earlier, on Feb. 25, 2015, he told Hugh Hewitt he would 'certainly' show his returns if necessary.

WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS (BRING SNACKS)

  • Any taxpayer can authorize release of tax information by signing IRS Form 8821, which has no exception for returns under audit.
  • FactCheck.org found no law or regulation preventing him from releasing his filed returns.
  • After the debate he told CNN his lawyers would not allow release. FactCheck.org's reading: 'won't', not 'can't'.

WHY THIS MATTERS

From 'certainly' to 'not until the audit': the same returns, a different excuse.

THE FACTS (SORRY)

FactCheck.org documented Trump's reversal on releasing returns and explained that an audit did not prevent publication.

Finding: MisleadingTechnically words. Technically.
