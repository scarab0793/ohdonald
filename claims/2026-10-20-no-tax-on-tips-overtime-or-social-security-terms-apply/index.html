Taxes · Oct 20, 2026 · Misleading

No tax on tips, overtime or Social Security? Terms apply.

WHEN & WHERE WAS IT SAID?

When: Sep 13, 2026

Where: Press gaggle at the Irish Open, Ireland

Original context / reporting ↗
THE CLAIM (BRACE YOURSELF)
No tax on tips, no tax on overtime, no tax on Social Security

WHAT HE SAID (WORD FOR WORD, SADLY)

He said there would be "no tax" on tips, overtime and Social Security. The archived wording is: no tax on tips, no tax on overtime, no tax on Social Security.

WHEN AND WHERE (THE RECEIPTS HAVE A CALENDAR)

A press gaggle at the Irish Open on Sept. 13, 2026, according to PolitiFact. FactCheck.org's October 2026 roundup of his midterm rallies also addressed the claim.

WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS (BRING SNACKS)

  • The tipped-income break phases out above $150,000 for single filers and $300,000 for joint filers.
  • The overtime break covers only overtime required under the Fair Labor Standards Act, and only the 'half' portion of time-and-a-half pay.
  • The Social Security provision is a $6,000 deduction for people 65 and older, not a repeal of tax on benefits.
  • All three breaks expire in 2028 unless Congress extends them.

WHY THIS MATTERS

These are real tax breaks with caps, income limits and expiry dates. 'No tax' is the slogan; the fine print is the law.

THE FACTS (SORRY)

The deductions have caps and income restrictions; the Social Security provision does not eliminate all taxes on benefits.
