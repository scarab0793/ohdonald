Cognitive tests · May 31, 2026 · False

A perfect cognitive-screening score is not an IQ score

WHEN & WHERE WAS IT SAID?

When: Not yet independently verified

Where: Not yet independently verified

The archive date is not necessarily the date the statement was made.

THE CLAIM (BRACE YOURSELF)
I scored a perfect 30 out of 30, considered ‘extreme intelligence.’

WHAT HE SAID (WORD FOR WORD, SADLY)

He said he scored "a perfect 30 out of 30," which he called "extreme intelligence." He described it as his fourth perfect test.

WHEN AND WHERE (THE RECEIPTS HAVE A CALENDAR)

Reported at the end of May 2026; PolitiFact's rating is dated June 3, 2026. The source does not give the exact venue or time, and we do not guess.

WHAT THE RECORD SHOWS (BRING SNACKS)

  • The Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) is a screening tool for cognitive impairment. Its creator and medical experts say it does not measure IQ.
  • A top score means no impairment was detected on that screen. It does not establish 'extreme intelligence'.
  • Trump called it his fourth perfect test, so it is inaccurate to say he had taken only one.
  • The archive takes no position on anyone's health. The claim checked is about what the test measures.

WHY THIS MATTERS

A thermometer reading of 98.6 does not prove you can run a marathon. Same logic, with a test about memory.

THE FACTS (SORRY)

Trump reported a 30/30 score on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment. Its creator and medical experts explain that MoCA screens for cognitive impairment; it does not measure IQ or establish 'extreme intelligence'. Trump described this as his fourth perfect test, so it is inaccurate to say he had taken only one test.

Finding: FalseBold. Wrong, but bold.
